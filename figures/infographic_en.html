Stronger Export–Import Performance
Drives National Economic Recovery
Through Manufacturing

The Indonesian government is stepping up its National Economic Recovery Program (PEN), which aims to revive economic activity that slowed during the COVID-19 pandemic. Alongside the program, stronger export and import performance is helping the economy recover.
According to theCoordinating Ministry for Economic Affairs

In May 2021, Indonesia's exports reached US$16.60 billion,1 up

58.76%

Indonesia's imports reached US$14.23 billion,1 up

68.68%

Manufacturing was the most stable sector under pandemic pressure, and it also contributed the most to exports.

Indonesia's export structure, August 20212
Indonesia's import structure, August 20212

Manufacturing output depends on a steady supply of imported raw materials and intermediate goods. Reliance on imports is still high, but these imports keep domestic industry running, which in turn boosts exports and income.

Indonesia's manufacturing exports and imports, year by year

Processed food and beverages is the sector with the highest export value.
Pasta and wheat flour are examples of manufactured products made from imported wheat.

Wheat imports, 2001–2020

Wheat imports reached US$2.6 billion in 2020, down 6% from 2019. The largest supplier was Australia (US$1.4 billion).3

Flour and pasta exports, 2001–2020

The largest pasta importer was Malaysia (US$86 million); for flour it was the Philippines (US$19 million).3

Food-processing machinery imports, 2001–2020

Most production machinery used in Indonesia is still imported, from countries such as China, the Netherlands and the United States.
During the national recovery, imports are needed to support manufacturing output. The hope is that Indonesia can eventually produce some commodities, such as wheat, on its own.
Sources: BPS (Statistics Indonesia), ekon.go.id (Coordinating Ministry for Economic Affairs), UN Comtrade. Charts are drawn from the UN Comtrade files in this repository (data/raw/, partner = World).
